# 11 · Growth & Marketing

A beautiful gallery only sells if people see it. Here's how to get those first
customers and keep momentum — none of it requires a marketing budget.

---

## Start with the people you know

The first sales almost always come from your own circle.
- Tell **family and friends** directly (text, call, in person) — not just a
  public post they might scroll past.
- Ask the proud ones to **share** it and to **post a photo** when their canvas
  arrives.
- Offer the first few buyers a small **launch discount** in exchange for a photo
  of the piece on their wall (instant social proof).

---

## Instagram (the natural home for photography)

1. Make a business IG account with the studio name.
2. Add it to `config.js` so it links in the footer:
   ```js
   contact: { instagram: "yourhandle", ... }
   ```
3. Post consistently:
   - The **photographs themselves** (this is the product — let them shine).
   - **Behind the scenes**: Mom shooting, the gear, the locations.
   - **Canvases in real rooms** (yours, friends', customers').
   - Short captions + the **website link in bio**.
4. Use a few relevant hashtags (`#fineartphotography`, `#canvasart`,
   `#[yourcity]photography`).
5. Put the **website link in the bio** and mention it in posts/stories.

> 📸 A "canvas on a real wall" photo sells better than the bare image. Order a
> sample (your test order!) and photograph it in a nice room.

---

## Make Google find you (SEO — already built in)

The site already includes the technical SEO basics (page titles, descriptions,
a sitemap, social-share images). To help further:
- After launch, submit the site to **Google Search Console**
  (search.google.com/search-console) — verify ownership and submit
  `yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml`.
- Use **descriptive photo titles and tags** in the catalog (Google reads them).
- Keep the **About** page personal and specific (real story, real place names).

---

## Other low-cost channels

- **Pinterest** — photography and home décor do very well there; pin your pieces
  linking back to product pages.
- **Local angle** — farmers markets, craft fairs, local cafés/galleries that hang
  art. A small printed card with a QR code to the site works wonders.
- **Word of mouth** — every shipped canvas is a billboard in someone's home.
  Include a small thank-you card with the website + IG (you can have Pictorem or
  yourself include one).
- **Email list (later)** — collect emails from interested visitors and send an
  occasional "new collection" note.

---

## Pricing & promotions (gentle)

- A **launch offer** (e.g. 15% off the first week) creates urgency.
- **Seasonal pushes**: holidays, Mother's/Father's Day, "art for your new home."
- **Bundles**: a discount on sets of 2–3 for a gallery wall.
- You control all pricing in `config.js` — no need for coupon code systems;
  adjust prices for a sale window, or arrange discounts manually via email/the
  order notes.

---

## Measure what works

Once analytics is on (see [10 · Maintenance](10-maintenance.md)), watch:
- Which **photos/collections** get the most views → make more like them.
- Where **visitors come from** (Instagram? Google? direct?) → lean into it.
- Where people **drop off** → smooth that step.

---

## The one thing that matters most

**Keep adding great photographs and showing them off.** The product *is* the
marketing. A steady stream of strong images, posted consistently and linked back
to the gallery, is what builds this into a real business over time.
